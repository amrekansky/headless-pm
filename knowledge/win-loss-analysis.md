# Win-Loss Analysis

## What it is

Win-loss analysis is a structured research methodology for understanding why deals are won or lost, and why customers churn or stay — by interviewing the people who made those decisions. It is one of the highest-signal inputs available to product and marketing teams because it captures real decision-making, not hypothetical preferences. Unlike NPS or satisfaction surveys, win-loss interviews surface the competitive dynamics, product gaps, and messaging failures that internal data cannot show.

The methodology applies to two distinct groups: (1) **won customers** — who chose you over alternatives; (2) **churned or lost prospects** — who chose a competitor or did nothing.

## When to use

- Win rate is declining and you don't know why
- Competitive pressure is increasing and you're losing to a specific competitor
- Product roadmap debates ("should we build X?") would benefit from direct customer signal
- Sales team has theories about why you lose but they're all different
- Post-launch: a new feature or pricing change had unexpected adoption (positive or negative)
- Quarterly: ongoing cadence to track shifts in competitive position

**Minimum viable program:** 5-10 interviews per quarter. Meaningful patterns emerge around 15-20 interviews with a specific research question.

## Core components / steps

### Who to Interview

| Group | Why to interview | What they reveal |
|---|---|---|
| Recent wins (last 90 days) | Decision is fresh; you have their contact | Why they chose you; what almost stopped them; what competitors fell short on |
| Recent losses (last 90 days) | Decision is fresh; you can still reach them | Why competitor won; what would have changed the outcome; messaging/product gaps |
| Churned customers (last 6 months) | Retention insight; specific product failures | Why they left; what they're using instead; where you fell short on ongoing value |
| Long-tenured customers | Expansion insight | Why they stay; why they expanded; what they'd miss most |

**Interview timing rule:** Within 90 days of the event. Beyond that, memory distorts and rationalization increases.

**Who should NOT conduct interviews:** the salesperson who ran the deal. Interviewees self-censor; they tell sales what they think they want to hear. Use PM, researcher, or a neutral third party.

**Sample size:** 15-20 interviews per research objective. For ongoing tracking: 5-10 per quarter, stratified across win/loss/churn.

### Interview Structure (60-minute format)

**Opening (5 min)**
- Explain purpose: "We're trying to understand how you made this decision — your honest input shapes our product."
- Confirm recording consent
- Establish that there's no sales agenda

**Decision context (15 min)**
- "Walk me through how this evaluation came about."
- "Who was involved in the decision? What was each person's role?"
- "What were the must-have requirements going in?"

**Competitive evaluation (15 min)**
- "Which other vendors did you seriously evaluate?"
- "What made it to the final shortlist? Why did others fall off?"
- "How did you compare [your product] vs [competitor]?"

**The decision (10 min)**
For wins:
- "What tipped the decision in our favor?"
- "What almost made you choose someone else?"

For losses:
- "What ultimately led you to choose [competitor]?"
- "What would we have needed to do or offer to win?"

**Product and experience (10 min)**
- "Were there any specific features or capabilities that were a dealbreaker?"
- "What was your experience during the trial/evaluation?"
- "What was missing that you wished existed?"

**Closing (5 min)**
- "Is there anything I haven't asked that you think we need to know?"
- "If you were giving advice to our product team, what's the one thing you'd tell them?"

### Synthesis Patterns

After 5-10 interviews, begin tagging themes:

1. **Tag each response** by category: price, feature gap, competitor strength, sales experience, trust/credibility, onboarding, integration, decision criteria
2. **Count frequency**: a theme mentioned by 3+ interviewees is a signal; mentioned by 7+ is a pattern
3. **Separate perceptions from facts**: "they said our API was too complex" is a perception worth acting on even if the API is technically fine
4. **Look for the delta between wins and losses**: what do winners emphasize that losers don't?

### How to Act on Findings

| Finding type | Owner | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Feature gap consistently blocks deals | PM | Add to roadmap with evidence attached |
| Pricing perceived as too high despite value | Marketing + PM | Repackage, change positioning, or address packaging |
| Competitor doing X better | PM + Competitive Intel | Competitive response strategy |
| Sales process confusion | Sales Enablement | Update sales training, collateral |
| Messaging not landing | Marketing | Revise positioning, homepage, sales deck |
| Trust/credibility problem | Marketing + CS | Case studies, references, certifications |

**The most common mistake:** producing a research report that sits in a Notion page. Win-loss insights must be routed to specific owners with specific actions and a follow-up date.

## Key questions to ask

- Are we interviewing wins AND losses in equal numbers? (Skewing to only losses creates a distorted picture)
- Who is conducting the interviews — is there a neutrality problem?
- Are we doing this within 90 days of the event?
- Have we defined in advance what question we're trying to answer?
- Is the output going to a specific person who will take a specific action?

## Common mistakes

- **Only interviewing losses** — you miss why you win, which is equally important for positioning
- **Sales conducting their own win-loss interviews** — biases responses; customers are polite to salespeople
- **Too few interviews to find patterns** — 3 interviews is anecdotes; 15+ is patterns
- **Asking "what features would you want?"** — future-looking feature requests are unreliable; ask about past decisions instead
- **Treating every interviewee insight as a product requirement** — one person's priority isn't everyone's priority; look for frequency
- **One-time research vs ongoing program** — a quarterly cadence detects competitive shifts early; one-time analysis is a snapshot

## Quick reference

```
Who: won deals, lost deals, churned customers (equal representation of wins/losses)
When: within 90 days of the decision event
Who interviews: PM, researcher, or neutral party — NOT the salesperson
How many: 15-20 per research objective; 5-10/quarter for ongoing tracking
Structure: context → evaluation → decision → product → closing advice
Synthesis: tag themes, count frequency, separate perceptions from facts
Output: route to specific owners with specific actions — not a report that sits in Notion
```

**The single best win-loss question:** "What almost made you choose differently?" — it surfaces the true decision factors that opened/closed the deal.
