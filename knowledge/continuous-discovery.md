# Continuous Discovery

## What it is
Continuous discovery is the practice of maintaining weekly touchpoints with customers — by the product team building the product — to run small research activities in pursuit of a desired outcome. Coined by Teresa Torres (producttalk.org), it replaces periodic big-bang discovery with a steady cadence of learning that informs decisions in real time.

## When to use
- When your team makes product decisions without regular customer input
- When discovery and delivery feel like separate phases (not concurrent)
- When you're unsure whether the opportunities you're working on actually matter to customers
- When your team needs a shared, visual artifact to align around what to build and why

## Core components

### 1. The Product Trio
Discovery is a team sport. PM, designer, and at least one engineer participate together — not just PM or UX in isolation. Engineers who witness customer pain directly commit to better solutions.

### 2. The Opportunity Solution Tree (OST)
A visual map with four layers:
```
Desired Outcome (business metric the team owns)
    └── Opportunity Space (customer needs, pains, desires)
            └── Solutions (product ideas that address opportunities)
                    └── Assumption Tests (fastest way to validate)
```
- **Root:** One clear desired outcome (e.g., "increase weekly active users")
- **Opportunities:** NOT features — they are customer needs, pains, desires discovered through interviews
- **Solutions:** Multiple competing ideas per opportunity; never jump to a single solution
- **Tests:** Broken-down assumptions, each testable faster than a full prototype

### 3. Weekly Interview Cadence
- **Target:** One customer interview per week, per product trio
- **Minimum viable start:** One interview per month, then increase
- **Key rule:** Automate recruiting — if you have to hustle to find customers, you won't sustain the cadence
- **Interview style:** Collect specific stories about past behavior ("Tell me about the last time you..."), never ask hypotheticals or what-ifs
- **Session length:** 30–60 minutes; can be as little as 15 minutes for a focused story

### 4. Assumption Testing (not full MVPs)
Break ideas into underlying assumptions, test the riskiest one first. Types of tests:
- **Desirability:** Do customers want this?
- **Viability:** Can we charge for it / does it fit our business?
- **Feasibility:** Can we build it well enough?
- **Usability:** Can customers use it without help?

## Key questions to ask
- What is the one desired outcome our team owns right now?
- What opportunities (customer needs/pains) would move that outcome?
- For each opportunity: how many customers experience it? How painful is it?
- For each solution: what assumptions must be true for this to work?
- What is the fastest, cheapest way to test the riskiest assumption?

## Common mistakes
- **Treating discovery as a phase:** Discovery never ends; it runs parallel to delivery
- **PM-only discovery:** Engineers and designers who skip interviews build to spec, not to need
- **Solution-first trees:** Teams map solutions they already decided to build instead of starting from opportunities
- **Interview as pitch:** Asking customers "Would you use X?" instead of collecting behavioral stories
- **Big-bang assumption tests:** Running full MVPs when a fake door or prototype test in a week would suffice
- **Too many outcomes:** Teams that own multiple desired outcomes at once scatter their opportunity mapping and can't prioritize

## Quick reference

**OST construction checklist:**
- [ ] One desired outcome at root
- [ ] Opportunities written as customer needs ("need to...", "struggle with...", "want to...")
- [ ] No solutions at opportunity level
- [ ] Multiple competing solutions per prioritized opportunity
- [ ] Riskiest assumption identified per solution
- [ ] Assumption test designed (not full build)

**Weekly discovery rhythm:**
```
Monday: Review OST, update with last week's learnings
Tuesday/Wednesday: Run customer interview (30-60 min)
Thursday: Debrief with trio, update opportunities/assumptions
Friday: Design one assumption test for next sprint
```

**Interview question patterns:**
- "Tell me about the last time you [problem area]..."
- "Walk me through what you did..."
- "What was hard about that?"
- "What did you do instead?"
