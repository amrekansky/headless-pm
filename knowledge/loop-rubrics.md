# Loop rubrics

Per-artifact quality checklists used by `/pm-loop` to decide whether an output holds up. Each rubric is a short list of pass/fail criteria. The loop verifies the artifact against the matching rubric, revises what fails, and re-verifies until every criterion passes or the safety cap is hit.

Pick the rubric by artifact type (frontmatter, filename, or content). If the type is unknown, use **Generic**.

## PRD
- Problem is evidenced, not asserted — tied to data, research, or a named user pain
- Jobs-to-be-done or user need is explicit
- Scope AND non-goals are both stated
- Success metrics are measurable (number / % / binary), with baseline and target
- Risks and open questions are named, not hidden
- An owner and a concrete next step exist

## Research synthesis
- Sample is described (who, how many, how recruited)
- Themes are grounded in evidence (quotes / observations), not the author's opinion
- Contradictions and disconfirming signals are surfaced, not smoothed over
- Assumptions are separated from findings
- A clear "so what" — implications for the product decision

## Roadmap
- Items map to active Objectives (no orphan work)
- Now / Next / Later (or equivalent) structure is explicit
- Dependencies and sequencing are stated
- Each item has a rationale, not just a title
- Stakeholder views or trade-offs are addressed where relevant

## Sprint plan
- A single, outcome-framed sprint goal
- Capacity is checked against committed scope
- Each item is prioritized by outcome alignment, not order of arrival
- Dependencies and blockers are flagged
- Definition of done is stated for the sprint

## Brief / stakeholder update
- Audience-tailored — leads with what that audience needs (exec: decision/ask; eng: context)
- One clear ask or decision, not a status dump
- Risks and changes since last update are surfaced
- Concrete next steps with owners and dates

## Competitive
- Claims are sourced, not asserted
- Compares on dimensions that matter to the buyer, not a feature laundry list
- Positioning gaps and our wedge are explicit
- Includes a "so what" — what we do differently as a result

## Persona / JTBD
- Built from evidence, not invented demographics
- Captures the job, the forces (push/pull/anxiety/habit), and the switch trigger
- Distinguishes segments by behavior/attitude, not just firmographics
- Names what this persona does NOT care about

## Metrics
- One North Star or primary metric is identified, with input metrics that predict it
- Each metric has a baseline and a target
- Counter-metrics / guardrails are named (what we must not break)
- The metric ties to an active Objective

## Generic (fallback)
- Complete — no obvious gaps or unanswered questions for its purpose
- Internally consistent — no section contradicts another
- Concrete — specifics over vague language; claims are grounded
- Actionable — a reader knows what to do or decide next
- Advances an active goal (when one exists) — otherwise flagged as drift
