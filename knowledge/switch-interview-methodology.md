# Switch Interview Methodology — Bob Moesta

## What it is

The Switch Interview (also called the Timeline Interview in demand-side sales) reconstructs the full decision journey a customer went through when switching FROM an old solution TO a new one. Unlike traditional customer interviews that focus on product features or satisfaction, the switch interview focuses on the causal forces that drove the customer's behavior change.

Developed by Bob Moesta and Chris Speak at The ReWired Group. Core insight: **customers don't "buy" products — they hire them to make progress in a specific situation.** Understanding what triggered the switch reveals the actual job-to-be-done.

## The 5-Stage Timeline

Each stage represents a phase in the customer's journey. The interviewer reconstructs the timeline chronologically by asking "what happened just before that?"

### Stage 1: First Thought

The moment the customer first realized they had a problem or desire. Usually NOT the moment they searched for a solution.

Key questions:
- "When did you first have the thought that you needed to do something about [problem]?"
- "What were you doing when that thought first hit you?"
- "Had you been thinking about it before? When did it become real?"

What to listen for: The "struggling moment" — the specific situation that created frustration. This is often the actual JTBD trigger.

### Stage 2: Passive Looking

The period of low-level awareness where the customer noticed signals that might help without actively searching.

Key questions:
- "In the weeks/months after that first thought, were you sort of keeping an eye out for things that might solve this?"
- "Did you notice any ads, articles, or conversations that seemed relevant?"
- "Did anyone recommend anything?"

What to listen for: Passive demand signals — what content, channels, and word-of-mouth touched them before they were "in market."

### Stage 3: Active Looking

The trigger that moved the customer from passive to active search. This is often a distinct event (a deadline, a failure, a conversation).

Key questions:
- "At what point did you decide you actually needed to find something?"
- "What made it urgent?"
- "What did you search for? What words did you use?"

What to listen for: The "deciding event" that creates urgency. This reveals the emotional intensity behind the switch and the messaging that will resonate.

### Stage 4: Deciding

The evaluation and selection phase. The customer is comparing options, often with anxiety about making the wrong choice.

Key questions:
- "What options did you look at? What did you consider?"
- "How did you narrow it down?"
- "What almost made you not choose [product]?"
- "Was there a moment of doubt? What was it?"

What to listen for: Anxieties and trade-offs. The "almost didn't" moments reveal the product's biggest friction points and the objections that appear in sales calls.

### Stage 5: Consuming (First Use / Hire Context)

The moment of first use and the customer's expectations. What did they hire the product to do?

Key questions:
- "Walk me through the first time you used it. What did you do?"
- "What did you expect would happen?"
- "What surprised you — positively or negatively?"
- "Looking back, what were you really hiring it to do?"

What to listen for: The actual job. Customers often reveal here that the "job" was different from the category the product is sold in.

## The Four Forces Framework

After completing the timeline, synthesize the interview into four forces that drove (or blocked) the switch:

| Force | Direction | Definition |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **Push** | Away from old | Frustration with the current solution |
| **Pull** | Toward new | Attraction to the new solution's promise |
| **Anxiety** | Away from new | Fear about the new solution |
| **Habit** | Toward old | Comfort with the familiar way |

For a switch to happen: Push + Pull must exceed Anxiety + Habit.

This framework explains why features that seem obvious don't drive adoption (Habit is too strong) and why "better" products don't automatically win (Anxiety blocks despite Pull).

## How to synthesize multiple switch interviews

After 6-8 interviews:
1. Map each interview to the Four Forces
2. Identify recurring Push triggers across interviews → this is your messaging hook
3. Identify recurring Anxieties → these are your objection-handling priorities
4. Identify Habit patterns → these inform your onboarding and change management strategy
5. Cluster by "First Thought" situation → each cluster is a potential market segment

## Common interviewing mistakes

**Starting with the product.** The switch interview should start with the customer's life and situation, not with your product. Starting with "how did you find us?" jumps to Stage 4 and misses the crucial Stages 1-3.

**Accepting the first answer.** Customers give sanitized, rational answers first. Ask "what happened just before that?" repeatedly to reach the real trigger.

**Ignoring the hire context.** Stage 5 (Consuming) is where the actual job reveals itself. Most interviewers stop at Stage 4 (Deciding) and miss what the customer was really trying to accomplish.

**Conflating features with forces.** A customer saying "I chose it because of feature X" is describing a Pull force — not the job. Ask "what were you trying to do when you used feature X?" to get to the job.

## Quick reference

Bob Moesta's 5-stage timeline interview reconstructs the customer's switch from old to new solution.

**5 stages:** First Thought (struggling moment) → Passive Looking (low-level awareness) → Active Looking (urgency trigger) → Deciding (evaluation + anxieties) → Consuming (first use / hire context).

**Four forces:** Push (away from old) + Pull (toward new) must exceed Anxiety (about new) + Habit (comfort with old) for a switch to happen.

**What each stage reveals:**
- First Thought → JTBD trigger and struggling moment
- Active Looking → messaging and channel strategy (what moved them to search)
- Deciding → anxieties and top objections
- Consuming → the actual job hired for

**Key technique:** Reconstruct chronologically. Always ask "what happened just before that?" to reach the real trigger.
